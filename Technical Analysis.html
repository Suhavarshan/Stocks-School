Technical Analysis – A Beginner’s Guide


What is Technical Analysis?

Technical Analysis is a method of studying a stock’s price movements, trading volume, and charts to understand market trends and possible future price movements.

Unlike fundamental analysis, which focuses mainly on a company’s business and financial performance, technical analysis focuses mainly on price and market activity.

Why is Technical Analysis Important?

Stock prices can move up and down based on buying and selling activity. Technical analysis helps traders and investors study these movements and identify possible patterns and trends.

It can help answer questions such as:

• Is the stock price moving upward or downward?

• Is there a clear trend?

• At what price levels have buyers or sellers been active?

• Is trading volume increasing or decreasing?

• What do the charts suggest about current market momentum?

Key Things To Understand


1. Price Charts

Charts display the movement of a stock's price over a period of time.

The most commonly used chart for technical analysis is the candlestick chart. Each candle can show the opening price, closing price, highest price, and lowest price for a particular period.

2. Trends

A trend shows the general direction in which the price is moving.

There are three basic types:

Uptrend– Prices generally move higher

Downtrend– Prices generally move lower.

Sideways– Prices move within a relatively limited range.

3. Support and Resistance

Support is a price level where buying interest may become stronger and can sometimes slow or stop a decline.

Resistance is a price level where selling interest may become stronger and can sometimes slow or stop a rise.

These levels are not guaranteed turning points.

4. Trading Volume

Volume shows how many shares are traded during a particular period.

Changes in volume can provide additional information about the strength of a price movement. However, volume should be studied together with price and other factors.

5. Moving Averages

A Moving Average calculates the average price over a selected period.

It can help reduce short-term price fluctuations and make the overall trend easier to observe.

6. RSI

RSI (Relative Strength Index) is a momentum indicator that measures the speed and magnitude of recent price movements.

Traders often use RSI to study momentum and identify conditions that may deserve further investigation.

7. Chart Patterns

Technical analysts study patterns that appear on price charts. Examples include:

• Double Top

• Double Bottom

• Triangle

• Head and Shoulders

These patterns can provide clues about possible market behaviour, but they do not guarantee what will happen next.

Simple Example

Suppose a stock has been making higher highs and higher lows for several weeks. This may indicate an uptrend.

If the price later reaches an important resistance level, a technical analyst may study the price action and volume to understand whether the trend is continuing or changing.

Fundamental Analysis focuses mainly on the company's business and financial health.

Technical Analysis focuses mainly on price movements, charts, volume, and market behaviour.

Both approaches have different purposes and can be studied separately or together.

Final Advice

Technical analysis is a tool for studying market behaviour. It does not predict the future with certainty.

A good learner should understand charts, trends, support and resistance, volume, and indicators before making conclusions based on technical analysis.
